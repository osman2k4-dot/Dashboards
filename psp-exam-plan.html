Exam facts (AACE official page, checked Oct 2026)

Format
Closed-book, computer-based. 5 hours maximum. 119 multiple-choice and compound-scenario questions plus one memo-writing assignment.
Eligibility
8 years industry-related experience, or 4 years experience + 4-year industry-related degree. Agreement to the AACE Canons of Ethics.
Fees
US$525 member / US$690 non-member, paid with the application, non-refundable. Resit US$260. Membership is optional but the member rate more than covers an individual membership.
Passing
AACE reports a pass/fail; third-party guides quote ~70% average across domains. Treat each domain as needing a solid pass, not just the total.
Recertification
Every 3 years via continuing-education units or re-examination.
Calculator
Allowed (battery-operated). Practise every CPM calculation by hand anyway, it is faster under time pressure.

Verify on AACE — PSP details and the Certification FAQs before applying; fees and the question count change from time to time.

Formula sheet — must be automatic by Week 12

Forward passEF = ES + Duration (zero-based) or ES + D − 1 (day-numbered). ES of successor = max(EF of predecessors + lag).
Backward passLS = LF − Duration. LF = min(LS of successors − lag).
Total floatTF = LS − ES = LF − EF. Critical path: longest path, TF = 0 (or lowest TF).
Free floatFF = min(ES of successors) − EF of activity.
RelationshipsFS, SS, FF, SF with lags/leads. Practise SS+lag and FF+lag passes, those are the usual traps.
PERT durationExpected = (O + 4M + P) / 6. Std dev = (P − O) / 6.
Earned valueEV = % complete × BAC. SV = EV − PV. CV = EV − AC. SPI = EV / PV. CPI = EV / AC.
ForecastsEAC = BAC / CPI (typical). ETC = EAC − AC. VAC = BAC − EAC. TCPI = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC).
ProductivityProductivity = Output / Input (e.g. m³ per man-hour). Duration = Quantity / (Crew rate × crews). Performance factor = Planned MH / Actual MH.
Schedule % completeDuration-, cost-, or physical-based; know when each is appropriate and how weighting rolls up through the WBS.
Resource levellingDelay non-critical activities within float first; levelling may extend the end date, smoothing never does.
Schedule compressionCrashing (add resources, cost up) vs fast-tracking (overlap, risk up). Crash cost slope = (Crash cost − Normal cost)/(Normal dur − Crash dur).

Memo-writing method (practise weekly from Week 13)

  1. Read the scenario twice. List every number, date and constraint given. Underline what the recipient actually asked for.
  2. Header: To / From / Date / Subject. Subject states the recommendation, e.g. "Recommendation: 3-week recovery plan for Unit 200 piping".
  3. Purpose (2 lines): why you are writing and the decision needed.
  4. Findings (bullets): facts from the scenario with the calculation shown (float, SPI, days late). No facts you were not given.
  5. Options & analysis: 2–3 options with schedule, cost and risk impact.
  6. Recommendation & next steps: one clear recommendation, who does what by when.
  7. Time box: 35–40 minutes max. Leave the memo to the end of a 5-h sitting only if you have practised that order.

Weekly rhythm that fits a site job

  • Weekdays: 4 × 1 h early morning or after shift — reading + 15 questions.
  • Friday / rest day: 1 × 3 h block — calculations, mock sections, memo drill.
  • Sunday evening: 30 min — log hours here, review wrong answers, set next week's topic.
  • If a week slips, do not stretch the plan: cut reading, keep the questions and the deliverable.
